\begin{abstract}
We measure how uniformly small language models use their representation space when reading 22 scheduled Indian languages and English. For every layer we compute IsoScore, average random cosine similarity, maximum explainable variance, and intrinsic dimension on 16{,}000 sampled tokens per language of the n-way parallel IN22-Gen benchmark. We compare two contrastively trained embedding models (EmbeddingGemma-300M, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) with two generative models (Gemma-3-1B-PT, Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base). The last layer before the final RMSNorm is close to rank one in EmbeddingGemma and highly clustered in Gemma-3-1B-PT because of massive activation dimensions; the norm removes most of this, so isotropy must be reported before and after it. After the norm, the embedding models are more isotropic than the generative models (token IsoScore 0.23 and 0.11 against 0.04 and 0.01), and this order holds for 19 of the 23 languages. Languages fall into four groups with similar layer-wise behaviour: English, 18 Indic languages, Bodo with Kashmiri, and Manipuri (Meitei script) with Santali (Ol Chiki script). The last group is the least isotropic in three of the four models.
\end{abstract}
