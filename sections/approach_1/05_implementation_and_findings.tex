% \paragraph{Pipeline Readiness}

Datasets, model families, and metrics are registered classes, so a new one is added without changing the runner. \texttt{dataset\_loader.py} downloads, caches, and aligns seven datasets. \texttt{model\_loader.py} wraps sentence-transformers and Hugging Face models behind one interface and captures the input of the final norm with \texttt{tie\_hidden\_state=False} flag. \texttt{inference.py} extracts all hidden states and the three views: token-level, mean-pooling sentence-level, and last-token pooing sentence-level. \texttt{isotropy\_metrics.py} implements isotropy metrics in torch. \texttt{run.py} runs and resumes experiments, and \texttt{plotting.py} draws per-layer metric and 3-D PCA plots. \texttt{scripts/report\_figures.py} draws every figure and table of this section from the saved results. Token-view results are complete for all four models on all 23 languages of IN22-Gen.

\paragraph{Preliminary Findings and Result Patterns}
Figures~\ref{fig:app1_depth} and~\ref{fig:app1_groups} and Tables~\ref{tab:app1_ablation} and~\ref{tab:app1_groups} show five patterns.

First, the final norm hides a nearly rank-one last layer. Before the norm, EmbeddingGemma has token IsoScore 0.001, average cosine 0.969, and MEV 0.871. After the norm, these are 0.228, 0.140, and 0.049. The norm breakdown on Hindi explains this (Table~\ref{tab:app1_norm_breakdown}). One dimension (731) reaches up to about 156{,}000, against about 7{,}000 for the next largest. Its learned gain $\gamma$ is 0.23 against a median of 10.7. Applying $\gamma$ alone lowers average cosine from 0.96 to 0.10, while the per-token division alone raises IsoScore from 0.0005 to 0.16. Gemma-3-1B-PT shows a weaker form: the norm lowers average cosine from 0.92 to 0.77. Qwen3-Embedding removes its massive dimension (35) inside the last layer instead. On Hindi, that layer's update points against the shared direction, so average cosine falls from 0.66 to 0.21 before the norm. Qwen3.5-Base changes least (0.549 to 0.550).

Second, after the norm, the embedding models are more isotropic than the generative models. Token IsoScore is 0.228 for EmbeddingGemma and 0.114 for Qwen3-Embedding, against 0.038 for Qwen3.5-Base and 0.013 for Gemma-3-1B-PT. Average cosine follows the same order: 0.14 and 0.20 against 0.55 and 0.77. The order holds for IsoScore in 19 of the 23 languages and for average cosine in 21 (Appendix~\ref{app:app1_values}). ID after the norm is also higher, 14.9 and 10.0 against 7.0 and 6.4. This matches the uniformity that contrastive training promotes \citep{wang-isola-2020-understanding}.

Third, the inner layers follow no common pattern by objective; each model has its own profile. In EmbeddingGemma, average cosine fluctuates between 0.45 and 0.86 up to layer 22, and layers 23 and 24 collapse towards one direction (average cosine 0.96 and 0.97, MEV 0.80 and 0.87). In Qwen3-Embedding, layers 3 to 15 have MEV above 0.99 in every language, and median IsoScore falls to $10^{-6}$; it then rises steadily by three orders of magnitude to $10^{-3}$ at layer 27. In Gemma-3-1B-PT, average cosine is at least 0.92 in every language from layer 6 to 20, and MEV peaks at 0.71 at layer 11. Qwen3.5-Base has the lowest average cosine (0.2 to 0.55) and the highest inner IsoScore (median 0.02 to 0.10), except at layers 7 and 15, where IsoScore drops to 0.006 and 0.009 and MEV rises to 0.35 and 0.29. In every model, ID drops from 12 to 47 at the embedding layer to a median of 2 to 4 after layer 1 and never exceeds 21, far below $d$.

Fourth, the language groups keep the same order in most models (Figure~\ref{fig:app1_groups}, Table~\ref{tab:app1_groups}). English is the most isotropic group after the norm in all four models. In Qwen3.5-Base it also has the lowest average cosine from layer 3 onwards (0.25 in inner layers against at least 0.38). Manipuri and Santali are the least isotropic group after the norm in three models and have the highest inner average cosine in three. In Qwen3.5-Base, their IsoScore stays between 0.002 and 0.011 at every depth, an order of magnitude below the other groups at most depths. After the norm, Bodo and Kashmiri lie between the 18 Indic languages and Manipuri and Santali in IsoScore in three models, and have the highest average cosine after the norm in both generative models (0.85 and 0.73). Inside the 18-language group, Dogri, Konkani, Maithili, Sanskrit, and Sindhi move towards Bodo and Kashmiri in the last layers of Qwen3.5-Base (average cosine 0.59 to 0.69 before the norm, against 0.46 to 0.52 for the other 13).

Fifth, language matters most in the generative models. After the norm, group means of average cosine span 0.36 to 0.85 in Gemma-3-1B-PT and 0.30 to 0.73 in Qwen3.5-Base, against 0.09 to 0.28 in EmbeddingGemma and 0.18 to 0.29 in Qwen3-Embedding. Gemma-3-1B-PT separates the groups only at the final norm: in its inner layers, all groups have average cosine between 0.92 and 0.94. Qwen3.5-Base separates them at every depth. These geometric patterns describe the representation space and do not by themselves measure task quality.

\paragraph{Limitations}
This report covers the token view only; the sentence views are being recomputed after the fixes described in Section~\ref{sec:app1_experimental_plan}. The Qwen pair differs in generation (Qwen3 against Qwen3.5) as well as objective, so Qwen3-0.6B-Base would be a cleaner baseline for Qwen3-Embedding. All results use one dataset, one $N$, and one seed. The language groups come from clustering 23 points, and the split inside the 18-language group is not stable. IN22-Gen sentences are translated from English, so translation effects are not yet separated from language effects.
