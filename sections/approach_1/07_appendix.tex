This appendix extends Section~\ref{sec:app1_implementation_findings} to every metric, model, and language. All results use the token view ($N = 16{,}000$) of IN22-Gen. In all plots, solid lines are embedding models and dashed lines generative models, markers distinguish the models, and the hollow marker is the last layer after the final norm.

\subsection{Language Groups for All Metrics}
\label{app:app1_groups}
Figure~\ref{fig:app1_groups_all} repeats Figure~\ref{fig:app1_groups} for all four metrics. The bands show that the 18 Indic languages stay close together in every model and metric. In Qwen3.5-Base, Manipuri and Santali also have a higher MEV than the other groups at most depths, so a single direction carries more of their variance.

\subsection{Per-Language Curves}
\label{app:app1_languages}
Figures~\ref{fig:app1_lang_isoscore} to~\ref{fig:app1_lang_id} show each language in its own panel with all four models, so models can be compared for the same language. Languages are ordered by group. The model order seen in the means holds language by language. Averaged over inner layers, Qwen3-Embedding has the lowest IsoScore in 22 of the 23 languages, Gemma-3-1B-PT the highest average cosine in all 23, and Qwen3.5-Base the lowest average cosine in all except Bodo and Manipuri, where Qwen3-Embedding is lower.

\subsection{Last-Layer Values per Language}
\label{app:app1_values}
Table~\ref{tab:app1_languages} lists the last layer of every language before and after the final norm. Three single-language effects are hidden by the group means. In EmbeddingGemma, Bengali has the lowest IsoScore after the norm (0.064), below Manipuri, while Nepali, Maithili, Dogri, Konkani, Sanskrit, and Sindhi have the highest among the Indic languages (0.29 to 0.36). In Gemma-3-1B-PT, Urdu has the highest IsoScore after the norm among the Indic languages (0.038). In Qwen3-Embedding, IsoScore after the norm separates scripts within the 18-language group: its Devanagari languages and Urdu reach 0.14 to 0.17, while the languages in other Brahmic scripts reach 0.08 to 0.12.

\subsection{Norm Breakdown}
\label{app:app1_norm}
Table~\ref{tab:app1_norm_breakdown} splits the final RMSNorm, $\mathrm{RMSNorm}(x) = \gamma \odot x / \mathrm{rms}(x)$, into its two parts for the two embedding models on Hindi. Average cosine is invariant to the per-token division, so only $\gamma$ changes it. In EmbeddingGemma, $\gamma$ alone removes the shared direction carried by dimension 731. In Qwen3-Embedding, the last layer has already removed most of it, and the norm mainly raises IsoScore.
