\begin{abstract}
Pretrained neural text embeddings exhibit geometric degeneracies, including origin displacement and spectral anisotropy, that impair cosine similarity search and semantic correlation. This work presents an empirical reproduction and benchmarking study evaluating training-free, parameter-free geometric post-processing transformations across modern sub-1B parameter architectures on MTEB v2 benchmarks. We investigate four models spanning dedicated contrastively trained encoders (google/embeddinggemma-300m, Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) and un-fine-tuned base causal autoregressive language models (google/gemma-3-1b-pt, Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base) under sequence mean and final token pooling. Across 49 evaluation tables spanning asymmetric retrieval, symmetric similarity, dimensional compression ladders, intrinsic geometry diagnostics, pooling penalties, and theoretical investigations, we observe: (1) regularized spectral whitening (Soft-ZCA) closes up to 51.0\% of the performance gap between base language models and dedicated encoders; (2) mean-subspace projection deflation (R2) outperforms direct mean subtraction in 80.6\% of comparisons through first-order parallel error cancellation; (3) adaptive spectral tempering (SpecTemp) resolves the dual-regime compression divergence between whitening and PCA, outperforming native Matryoshka prefix slicing at 12x/16x compression; and (4) causal autoregressive pooling penalties scale strictly with sequence length, collapsing by over 45 Spearman points on long contexts.
\end{abstract}
