\begin{abstract}
Pretrained neural text embeddings exhibit geometric degeneracies, including origin displacement and spectral anisotropy, that impair cosine similarity search and semantic correlation. This work presents an empirical reproduction and benchmarking study evaluating training-free, parameter-free geometric post-processing transformations across modern sub-1B parameter architectures on MTEB v2 benchmarks. We investigate four models spanning dedicated contrastively trained encoders (google/embeddinggemma-300m, Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) and un-fine-tuned base causal autoregressive language models (google/gemma-3-1b-pt, Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base) under sequence mean and final token pooling. We observe: (1) regularized spectral whitening (Soft-ZCA) closes up to 51.0\% of the performance gap between base language models and dedicated encoders, while lowering the scores of the dedicated encoders themselves; (2) mean-subspace projection deflation (R2) outperforms direct mean subtraction in 80.6\% of comparisons, but by small margins; (3) for dedicated encoders, adaptive spectral tempering (SpecTemp) avoids the large-dimension loss of whitening and matches or beats native Matryoshka prefix slicing in most cases at 12x to 16x compression; and (4) final token pooling stays close to mean pooling on short sentences but falls by over 45 Spearman points on average on long texts.
\end{abstract}
