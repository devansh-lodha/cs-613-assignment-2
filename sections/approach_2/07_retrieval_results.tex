\section{Information Retrieval Benchmarks}
\label{sec:retrieval_results}

Information Retrieval (IR) tasks evaluate asymmetric search, where an incoming user query retrieves relevant document passages from a large candidate corpus. In this setting, the transformation parameters (empirical mean vector $\mu_D$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_D$, and orthogonal projection matrix $W_D$) are estimated strictly on the document corpus representations $D \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$. The identical transformation parameters are applied to query vectors $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$:
\begin{equation}
D' = (D - \mu_D) W_D, \quad Q' = (Q - \mu_D) W_D
\end{equation}
Both transformed matrices are L2-normalized to unit length: $D' \leftarrow D' / \|D'\|_2$, $Q' \leftarrow Q' / \|Q'\|_2$. Candidate documents are ranked by inner product $S = Q' (D')^\top$, and the top 100 retrieved candidates per query are evaluated using \texttt{pytrec-eval}.

Tables~\ref{tab:arguana_ndcg} to~\ref{tab:scidocs_recall} in Appendix~\ref{app:app2} report nDCG@10, MRR@10, and Recall@100 for every model and method on ArguAna, FiQA2018, and SCIDOCS (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}).

\paragraph{The base-to-dedicated gap depends on the dataset.}
Raw base models trail both dedicated encoders on every dataset, but by very different margins. On ArguAna, mean-pooled base models reach about two thirds of the dedicated nDCG@10. On FiQA2018 and SCIDOCS they reach only about a tenth. On ArguAna, Recall@100 of the dedicated encoders is close to 1 under every method, so it leaves little room to separate methods.

\paragraph{Soft-ZCA helps base models and hurts dedicated encoders.}
For both mean-pooled base models, Soft-ZCA gives the highest nDCG@10, MRR@10, and Recall@100 of all methods on all three datasets. Its relative gain is larger where the raw score is lower: mean-pooled nDCG@10 rises by about a fifth on ArguAna, by over half on FiQA2018, and more than doubles on SCIDOCS. It also raises last-token nDCG@10 for both base models on every dataset. The same transform lowers both dedicated encoders on every dataset and metric, by about 0.03 to 0.07 nDCG@10. Even after Soft-ZCA, every base model stays well below the raw dedicated encoders.

\paragraph{The other transforms barely move dedicated encoders.}
Standardization, R1, R2, and ABTT-1 to ABTT-3 change dedicated scores by at most about 0.02 on any IR metric, in both directions. Deflating a random direction (\texttt{rand}) changes no score of any model by more than 0.002. Mean centering without renormalization (\texttt{mc}) lowers dedicated scores in most cases, but raises EmbeddingGemma on ArguAna.

\paragraph{Removing the mean direction does not help base models.}
For mean-pooled base models, R1 and R2 slightly lower the average nDCG@10 on all three datasets. Only standardization and Soft-ZCA raise it on all three. ABTT-3 helps on ArguAna and FiQA2018, but on SCIDOCS every deflation method (R1, R2, and ABTT-1 to ABTT-3) falls below the raw baseline.

\paragraph{Renormalization matters most for mean-pooled base models.}
Without the final L2 normalization, mean centering (\texttt{mc}) cuts the mean-pooled base nDCG@10 by about a third on ArguAna and by about two thirds on FiQA2018 and SCIDOCS. The same centering followed by normalization (R1) stays much closer to the baseline.

\paragraph{Last-token pooling is the weakest setting.}
Last-token base models score far below mean pooling on every dataset, and post-processing does not close the gap (Section~\ref{sec:ablation_results}). The extreme case is Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base on FiQA2018, where no method lifts nDCG@10 above 0.005.
