\section{Semantic Textual Similarity Benchmarks}
\label{sec:similarity_results}

Semantic Textual Similarity (STS) evaluates symmetric pairwise sentence matching. Given sentence pairs $(S_1, S_2) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ originating from a shared linguistic domain, both sentence sets are pooled jointly to estimate a shared calibration mean $\mu$ and covariance transform $W$. Transformed sentences are projected onto the unit hypersphere:
\begin{equation}
S_1' = \frac{(S_1 - \mu)W}{\|(S_1 - \mu)W\|_2}, \quad S_2' = \frac{(S_2 - \mu)W}{\|(S_2 - \mu)W\|_2}
\end{equation}
Row-wise cosine similarity $\text{sim}_i = (S_{1, i}')^\top S_{2, i}'$ is evaluated against human ratings using Spearman rank correlation ($\rho \times 100$) and Pearson linear correlation ($r \times 100$).

Tables~\ref{tab:sickr_spearman} to~\ref{tab:stsb_pearson} in Appendix~\ref{app:app2} report both correlations for every model and method on SICK-R, STS22.v2, and STSBenchmark (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}).

\paragraph{The base-to-dedicated gap depends on the dataset.}
On the short-sentence datasets STSBenchmark and SICK-R, raw mean-pooled base models trail the dedicated encoders by more than 30 Spearman points. On the long-text STS22.v2 the gap is under 10 points.

\paragraph{Soft-ZCA gives base models the best rank correlation.}
Soft-ZCA gives the highest Spearman $\rho$ for every base model and pooling mode on all three datasets. Its gain on mean pooling is largest on STSBenchmark and smallest on STS22.v2, where the initial gap was smallest. Pearson $r$ does not always agree. On SICK-R, ABTT gives higher Pearson $r$ than Soft-ZCA for every base model and pooling mode. On STS22.v2 with last-token pooling, Soft-ZCA roughly doubles Spearman $\rho$ but leaves the average Pearson $r$ almost unchanged, while ABTT-3 raises both.

\paragraph{Soft-ZCA hurts dedicated encoders, most on SICK-R.}
Soft-ZCA lowers both dedicated encoders on SICK-R and STSBenchmark on both metrics, by about 9 to 16 points on SICK-R and 3 to 5 points on STSBenchmark. On STS22.v2 its effect on Spearman $\rho$ is small and model-dependent (EmbeddingGemma rises, Qwen3-Embedding falls), but Pearson $r$ falls for both. The other full-dimension transforms change dedicated Spearman $\rho$ by at most about 2.5 points, in both directions, and a few give small gains, such as R2 on STSBenchmark for both encoders.

\paragraph{The ABTT pattern differs by dataset.}
For base models, removing more principal components keeps helping on STSBenchmark, where every base model and pooling mode improves with each added component. On SICK-R, nearly all of the gain comes from removing the first component. On STS22.v2, ABTT leaves the mean-pooled average below the raw baseline but raises last-token scores sharply.

\paragraph{Renormalization matters, and the random control changes nothing.}
Without the final L2 normalization (\texttt{mc}), every model scores lower than with it (R1) on every STS dataset, by up to about 17 Spearman points. Deflating a random direction (\texttt{rand}) changes no correlation of any model by more than 0.05 points.

\paragraph{Last-token pooling fails only on long texts.}
On SICK-R and STSBenchmark, raw last-token pooling stays within about 5 Spearman points of mean pooling, but on STS22.v2 it falls far below it for both base models. Section~\ref{sec:ablation_results} examines this in detail.
