\section{Dimensional Compression Ladders}
\label{sec:compression_results}

Dimensional compression evaluates representation retention when high-dimensional embeddings ($d \in \{768, 1024, 1152\}$) are compressed into compact subspaces $k \in \{512, 256, 128, 64\}$. We evaluate five compression algorithms: coordinate prefix slicing (\texttt{prefix}), unweighted principal component projection (\texttt{pca}), truncated variance-equalizing whitening (\texttt{whitening}), adaptive spectral tempering (\texttt{spectemp}), and uniform random coordinate subsampling (\texttt{random\_truncation}).

\input{sections/approach_2/tables/compression/arguana_compression_recall.tex}

\paragraph{Analysis of ArguAna Compression}
\begin{itemize}
\item Broad Subspace ($k=512$): Prefix slicing retains 99.96\% of native nDCG@10 on dedicated models; PCA retains 99.56\%; SpecTemp retains 99.45\%. Standard whitening drops to 90.68\% retention (-9.32\% loss) due to noise tail amplification.
\item Compact Subspace ($k=64$): PCA retains 91.69\% nDCG@10 on dedicated models, while SpecTemp retains 89.33\%, whitening retains 87.69\%, and prefix slicing drops to 85.92\%. PCA outperforms prefix slicing by +5.77 percentage points, demonstrating that spectral rotation concentrates argumentative variance more effectively than fixed coordinate slicing.
\item Base Causal Models: Whitening at $k=512$ achieves 129.12\% retention on nDCG and 132.97\% on recall, overcoming initial representation collapse.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis of FiQA2018 Compression}
\begin{itemize}
\item Broad Subspace ($k=512$): SpecTemp retains 99.82\% of native nDCG@10 and 99.75\% of native MRR@10 on dedicated models. Whitening suffers steep degradation (86.92\% nDCG retention and 89.76\% MRR retention). SpecTemp sets $\gamma \approx 0.05$, successfully matching PCA without noise inflation.
\item Compact Subspace ($k=64$): SpecTemp establishes the top retention on dedicated models (75.73\% nDCG@10 and 77.50\% MRR@10), outperforming native Matryoshka prefix slicing (70.17\% nDCG and 73.19\% MRR) by over 5 percentage points.
\item Base Model Inversion: On base models, whitening at $k=512$ elevates nDCG@10 to 174.02\% and Recall@100 to 183.68\% of native performance, proving that post-hoc variance equalization eliminates financial vocabulary noise.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis of SCIDOCS Compression}
\begin{itemize}
\item Noise-Filtering Inversion ($k=512$): On dedicated models, both SpecTemp (100.71\% nDCG, 100.49\% MRR, 101.85\% Recall) and PCA (100.18\% nDCG, 101.57\% Recall) exceed full-rank uncompressed representations, confirming that the trailing dimensions encode citation noise.
\item Extreme Compression Advantage ($k=64$): SpecTemp preserves 74.55\% nDCG@10 and 76.18\% MRR@10 on dedicated models, outperforming prefix slicing (62.64\% nDCG and 67.54\% MRR) by +11.91 and +8.64 percentage points. On Recall@100, PCA and SpecTemp outperform prefix slicing by over +18 percentage points (88.57\% and 87.67\% vs 69.56\%).
\item Base Model Coverage: Whitening at $k=512$ achieves 214.50\% recall retention on base models, more than doubling candidate capture.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis of SICK-R Compression}
\begin{itemize}
\item High Retention Across Ladder: On short sentences, all spectral methods retain over 96.5\% of native correlation down to $k=64$. SpecTemp achieves the highest retention at $k=64$ (98.66\% Spearman and 98.77\% Pearson).
\item Whitening Noise at $k=512$: Standard whitening drops to 86.90\% Spearman retention and 81.84\% Pearson retention on dedicated models, while SpecTemp maintains 99.66\% and 100.11\%.
\item Base Model Recovery: Base causal models gain from spectral compression, reaching 119.59\% Spearman retention under whitening and 108.26\% under SpecTemp at $k=512$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis of STS22.v2 Compression}
\begin{itemize}
\item Long-Document Whitening Collapse: On ~2,048 token contexts, whitening on dedicated models collapses to 52.07\% Pearson retention at $k=512$. SpecTemp sets $\gamma = 0.00$, tracking PCA perfectly (99.56\% retention). Truncating low-variance noise dimensions at $k=64$ allows whitening to recover to 94.89\% retention.
\item Base Model Recovery: Whitening achieves 155.09\% Spearman retention at $k=512$ and 128.74\% at $k=64$ on base models, while SpecTemp preserves 116.14\% at $k=64$, significantly outperforming unweighted PCA (83.69\%).
\end{itemize}
