\section{Intrinsic Space Geometry Diagnostics}
\label{sec:geometry_results}

Intrinsic space geometry diagnostics quantify representation space distributions across five metrics: centroid norm ($\|\mu\|_2$), average pairwise cosine similarity, Maximum Explainable Variance of the leading eigenvalue (MEV Top-1), Normalized Intrinsic Dimensionality (NID), and global isotropy (IsoScore). For IR they are computed on the document embeddings, and for STS on all sentences of both sets.

Tables~\ref{tab:arguana_geometry} to~\ref{tab:stsb_geometry_comp} in Appendix~\ref{app:app2} report all five metrics for every model under the full-dimension transforms and under compression to $k=128$.

\paragraph{Raw base embeddings are far more crowded than dedicated ones.}
On every dataset, every base model and pooling mode has a larger centroid norm and a higher average cosine than either dedicated encoder, with no overlap between the two tiers. IsoScore is lower for base models on every dataset except SICK-R, where Qwen3-Embedding is level with the last-token base models.

\paragraph{Pooling mode changes the shape of the crowding.}
For both base models on every dataset, mean pooling gives a larger centroid norm and a higher average cosine than last-token pooling. Last-token pooling instead concentrates more variance in a single principal direction (higher MEV) on ArguAna, FiQA2018, and STSBenchmark for both models. On SICK-R the reverse holds, and on SCIDOCS and STS22.v2 the two pooling modes are close or mixed.

\paragraph{Centering removes the offset, but only Soft-ZCA spreads the variance.}
Standardization, R1, R2, ABTT, and Soft-ZCA all bring the centroid norm below 0.08 and the average cosine close to zero for every model. R1 and R2 change MEV, NID, and IsoScore only slightly. ABTT-3 and standardization lower MEV for every base model on every dataset, but far less than Soft-ZCA. Soft-ZCA gives the highest NID and IsoScore of all full-dimension methods for every model on every dataset. The \texttt{mc} rows have exactly the baseline MEV, NID, and IsoScore, since shifting all vectors by the same mean leaves their covariance unchanged.

\paragraph{Higher isotropy goes with better scores only for base models.}
Soft-ZCA gives the dedicated encoders their highest IsoScore on every dataset, yet it lowers their task scores in every case except EmbeddingGemma on STS22.v2 Spearman $\rho$ (Sections~\ref{sec:retrieval_results} and~\ref{sec:similarity_results}). For base models, the same transform gives both the highest isotropy and the best nDCG@10 and Spearman $\rho$.

\paragraph{Compression methods order cleanly by isotropy.}
At $k=128$, whitening reaches an IsoScore of at least 0.88 for every model on every dataset. SpecTemp always falls between PCA and whitening. Prefix slicing and random truncation do not subtract the mean, and they keep centroid norms between about 0.41 and 0.97.

\paragraph{STS22.v2 is the least isotropic after Soft-ZCA.}
Soft-ZCA reaches an NID of only 0.82 to 0.89 on STS22.v2, lower than on any other dataset, where it is at least 0.91 for every model.
